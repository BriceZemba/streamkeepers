# Season simulation: where do volunteer checks go?

> **Simulated volunteers, real sites.** The 106 OneAquaHealth research sites, their lab health-risk scores, lab dates and population near the water are real (ENORA API snapshot). The volunteers and their choices are a model, described below. These numbers show how the scoring rules steer effort *under these assumptions*; they are not field results.

Generated by `npm run simulate` (deterministic, 200 paired runs per scenario, seeds 1–200). Mission values come from the app's own `missionValue()`.

## Findings

1. **Fewer forgotten streams.** Value-priced missions leave fewer sites unchecked in a season than classic flat points, at the same median trip length (2.3 km): 11% → 7% of sites unchecked with 20 volunteers per city, and 31% → 24% with 10 per city (23% fewer unchecked sites).
2. **High-risk sites are checked more often at least once:** 71% → 79% with 10 volunteers per city, 91% → 96% with 20.
3. **Most of the gain comes from pricing by coverage; the OAH data adds a smaller part.** Without OAH data the same code reaches 75% coverage and 76% high-risk coverage (10 volunteers per city), versus 76% and 79% with it.
4. **Points do not pull volunteers to distant high-risk sites.** The share of all checks made at high-risk sites barely moves (23% → 24%), even with a three times larger risk weight (see what-if). Volunteers check streams near home; reaching far high-risk sites needs other levers (lab visits, organised outings).
5. **Crowding at the busiest sites is unchanged** (26% → 25% of checks on the 10% busiest sites): popular places stay popular; the gain is at the other end, on sites nobody visited.

Total checks per season are the same for every rule (≈327): the rules only change *where* volunteers go. Ranges are the 2.5–97.5 percentiles over 200 runs.

## How much do volunteers care about points? (sensitivity)

`betaPoints` is the assumption that matters most: how strongly a volunteer's choice follows points (0 = points are ignored).

| betaPoints | Classic: sites checked | StreamKeepers: sites checked | Without OAH data: sites checked | Classic: high-risk | StreamKeepers: high-risk | Without OAH data: high-risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 89% | 89% | 89% | 91% | 91% | 91% |
| 0.5 | 89% | 91% | 91% | 91% | 94% | 93% |
| 1 (default) | 89% | 93% | 93% | 91% | 96% | 94% |
| 2 | 89% | 96% | 95% | 91% | 98% | 97% |

At betaPoints = 0 all three rules give identical results (checked by a unit test): the scoring cannot help if people ignore it.

## What-if: the coordinator's lab-risk weight

The default weights add at most 30 points for lab health risk. That turns out to be too weak to move much effort towards high-risk sites: distance and popularity dominate. Because the weights are a coordinator setting, this table shows what a stronger risk weight would do, and what it costs in travel. The headline above keeps the default.

| Lab-risk weight | Sites checked | High-risk quartile checked | Share of checks at high-risk sites | Median trip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 93% | 95% | 24% | 2.3 km |
| 30 (default) | 93% | 96% | 24% | 2.3 km |
| 60 | 94% | 96% | 24% | 2.3 km |
| 90 | 94% | 96% | 25% | 2.3 km |

Classic flat points for reference: 23% of checks at high-risk sites.

## Model and assumptions

- **Sites:** 106 OAH research sites in 5 cities. Citizen-created sites are left out: they are scattered across Europe with no local volunteer pool to model.
- **Highest-risk quartile:** sites whose OAH lab health-risk score is ≥ the 75th percentile (96 sites have a score).
- **Volunteers:** 20 per city, living near sites weighted by population (people live where people live), ±1.5 km.
- **Season:** 13 weeks; each week a volunteer checks one stream with probability 0.25 (≈3.3 checks each).
- **Choice:** softmax over sites within 15 km of utility = −distance/2 km + 1 × popularity (population percentile) + betaPoints × points/100.
- **Classic rule:** every check is worth 10 points, so points don't steer the choice.
- **StreamKeepers:** the app's mission value (lab staleness, season gap, lab risk, people nearby, divided by 1 + checks already made this season), recomputed after every check.
- **Without OAH data (ablation):** the same code with lab dates, lab risk and population removed. What remains is the season gap and the crowding discount.
- **Not modelled:** adopted streams, reminders, teams, weather, safety or access, volunteers dropping out. Every check is assumed to pass the quality gate.

## Limits

- The behaviour parameters are assumptions, not measured. The sensitivity table shows how results move with the key one.
- Lab staleness is at its cap for every site (all public lab campaigns are 2023–24), so in this data it adds the same points everywhere and does not steer choices.
- Popularity is proxied by population near the site, the only OAH variable available for it.
